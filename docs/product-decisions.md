# Product & Engineering Decisions

This system intentionally focuses on the four workflows specified in the assessment:
enrolment, fees & payments, assessment submission, and marksheet/results. It is
not a general-purpose ERP, LMS, or accounting system — features outside those
four workflows (attendance tracking, timetabling, HR, bulk import, etc.) are
deliberately out of scope, to preserve depth over breadth within the assessment
window.

Each section below follows the same shape: **what the brief left open, what we
decided, and why.**

## Actors and permissions

The brief specifies Staff and Student but not what each can do. We defined it
explicitly and enforce it in code (not just hidden in the UI):

- **Staff**: full CRUD on students, programmes, and assessments; record
  payments; enter, edit (with the published-grade restriction below), and
  publish/withhold grades.
- **Student**: view their own record, balance, and payment history; submit
  and resubmit their own assessments; view only their own published results.
  Cannot view another student's data, create assessments, enter grades, or
  publish results.

There's still no real login — the brief allows a simple role toggle — but the
toggle sets a session cookie that's checked both in middleware (blocks
loading the wrong pages) and independently inside every API route (blocks
calling the wrong endpoint directly, bypassing the UI). See
`src/lib/session.ts` and `src/lib/api-guard.ts`.

## Student identity

**Student ID generation.** Format: `SMS-{enrolmentYear}-{sequence}`.
The sequence is a single global counter, generated by an atomic
`INSERT ... ON CONFLICT ... RETURNING` against a dedicated `IdCounter` row
(`src/lib/registry.server.ts`) — not by counting existing students, which is
race-condition-prone under concurrent enrolments. The counter never resets
per year (so `SMS-2026-0412` and `SMS-2027-0413` can coexist) and pads to 4
digits but grows naturally past 9999 rather than wrapping. Student IDs are
immutable and never reused, including for withdrawn students.

**Email uniqueness and normalization.** Email is treated as a unique
identifier for this simplified system, normalized to lowercase before
storage/comparison so `Alice@x.com` and `alice@x.com` aren't treated as
different people.

**Date of birth.** Must not be in the future, and must indicate the student
is at least 5 years old as of January 1st of the enrolment year. This is a
sanity check against obviously-wrong data entry (typoed years, swapped
day/month) — not a real admissions-age policy, which the brief doesn't
define and we didn't want to invent.

**Full name is a single field**, not split into first/last/middle, since the
brief says "full name" and splitting would impose an assumption about name
structure the brief doesn't ask for.

**No hard deletion.** Students are never deleted through normal workflows —
only their `status` changes (Enrolled/Deferred/Withdrawn/Completed). A
Registry system's job is largely to preserve historical academic and
financial records, not discard them.

## Fees & payments

**Fee snapshot, not a live reference.** `Student.assignedFee` is captured
from the programme's fee at enrolment time (with an optional staff
override), rather than the student's balance being calculated against
`Programme.feeAmount` directly. If a programme's fee later changes, already-
enrolled students keep what they were actually charged. The seed data
demonstrates this directly: Sadia enrolled when Computing's fee was $4,000;
the programme fee was later raised to $4,500; her `assignedFee` stays $4,000.

**Outstanding balance** = `assignedFee − sum(payments)`, computed on read,
never stored as a separate field or a paid/unpaid flag — this is what lets
partial payments work correctly and avoids two independently-updated numbers
drifting out of sync.

**Overpayments are rejected.** If a payment would push the balance below
zero, the API returns a 409 rather than accepting it. The brief doesn't
define credit/refund handling, and modeling student credit correctly is a
meaningfully bigger feature than this assessment calls for — rejecting is
the simpler, defensible choice, clearly stated so it isn't a silent
limitation.

**Payment recording is concurrency-safe.** Two staff members recording
payments against the same student at the same instant could otherwise both
read "balance = $500", both allow a $500 payment, and jointly overdraw the
account by $500. Payment creation runs inside a `SERIALIZABLE` Postgres
transaction (`src/app/api/students/[id]/payments/route.ts`); if that race
actually occurs, Postgres aborts one of the two transactions with a
serialization failure, which we surface as a 409 asking the client to retry
— rather than silently allowing the overdraw.

**Payment reference numbers are unique**, which doubles as a lightweight
idempotency safeguard against accidentally recording the same transaction
twice (e.g. a double-click).

**Payments are immutable** — no edit or delete endpoint exists. Financial
history shouldn't be silently rewritten; a real system would add explicit
reversal transactions rather than deletion, but that's beyond this scope.

**Overdue is a defined state, not just "balance > 0."** The brief asks for
"overdue" but never defines it, so we added `feeDueDate` on Student
(defaulted to 30 days after enrolment, staff-editable). A student is overdue
when `feeDueDate` has passed *and* they still owe money — a student who
enrolled yesterday and hasn't paid yet is outstanding, not overdue.
Withdrawn/deferred students are still included in overdue checks, since they
may still owe money and a real Registry office keeps chasing that balance
regardless of enrolment status.

## Assessments & submissions

**Module is a plain string field**, not a relational entity. A full
Module/Course/Semester model would be more "correct" in a real institution
but is out of scope for what four Registry workflows need.

**"Open" is defined implicitly by the deadline**, not a separate open/closed
flag — because the brief explicitly says late submissions are still
accepted (just flagged), there's no point in the assessment ever being fully
"closed" to first-time submissions.

**Resubmission is only allowed before the deadline.** A first-time
submission after the deadline is still accepted (and flagged late); but once
something has been submitted, it can't be replaced after the deadline. This
reading reconciles two brief statements that could otherwise seem to
conflict ("late submissions are accepted" vs. "allow resubmission before the
deadline").

**File validation**: extension, browser-reported MIME type, and a 10MB size
cap (arbitrary but explicit, since the brief doesn't specify one). None of
these fully prevent a determined attacker (MIME type is client-supplied),
but together they catch honest mistakes cheaply; true content-sniffing is
out of scope.

**Files are stored on local disk**, not object storage. This is a demo-
appropriate simplification — most serverless hosts don't persist local disk
writes across deployments — isolated behind `src/lib/storage.ts` so it's a
one-file change later.

**A student can only submit against an assessment open to their own
programme** (or one open to all programmes) — verified server-side from
their session, not from anything the client claims.

## Grades & results

**Grades are whole numbers, 0–100.** The brief says "numeric grade" without
specifying decimals; we deliberately kept grades as integers rather than
adding decimal precision, since the brief doesn't ask for it and percentage-
based whole-number grading is the more common default — this is a case
where we chose *not* to add complexity a stronger AI-generated first draft
suggested.

**A grade only exists once a submission exists** — `Grade.submissionId` is a
required 1:1 relation. You can't grade work that was never handed in.

**"Not graded," "withheld," and "published" are three distinct, real
states**, not two:
- No `Grade` row at all → **not graded**.
- `Grade` exists, `publishedAt` is `null` → **withheld** (staff can see the
  score; the student cannot).
- `Grade.publishedAt` is a timestamp → **published**, visible to the
  student, and the timestamp itself is a small audit trail of when.

**Published grades are protected from silent edits.** If staff tries to
change the score of an already-published grade while keeping it published,
the API rejects it (409) and asks them to withhold it first. This exists
because we found a real bug during development: an earlier version of the
"Save" button unconditionally sent `published: false`, meaning saving a
score change would silently unpublish an already-visible result without
anyone intending that. The fix is two-layered: the UI now preserves the
current publish state on save, and the API independently refuses to let a
score change ride through while `published: true`, so the rule holds even
if a future UI change reintroduces the same mistake.

**Publication is per-student**, via each Grade's own `publishedAt`, not a
single per-assessment switch — matching "publish or withhold results per
student" in the brief literally, and because a real member of staff
plausibly wants to publish some students' results before others are graded.

## What we deliberately did not build

Documenting non-goals is as much a product decision as documenting what we
did build:

- **Real authentication.** The brief explicitly allows a role toggle; we
  built one with a server-checked session cookie, but it's not
  cryptographically trustworthy (see `src/lib/session.ts`). A production
  system needs actual login.
- **Multi-currency support.** All amounts are treated as a single implicit
  currency. Worth a `currency` field in a real system; not worth the added
  complexity here since the brief never mentions currency.
- **Submission attempt history.** Resubmitting overwrites the current
  submission rather than keeping every prior attempt. The brief asks for
  resubmission, not a version history of it.
- **Audit trail of *who* performed an action** (`createdBy`/`updatedBy`).
  There's no real user identity to attach to actions beyond the role
  toggle, so this would be cosmetic rather than a real audit trail.
- **URL-synced filters/pagination state** on the student list. The search,
  filter, and pagination state lives in React state, not the URL — meaning
  filters aren't shareable/bookmarkable. A reasonable next step, not
  implemented here.
- **Bulk grade publication.** Publishing is one submission at a time,
  matching the brief's literal "per student" wording. A "publish all" button
  would be a nice addition but wasn't requested.
- **CSV import/export, notifications, a payment gateway, soft-delete for
  programmes.** All reasonable production features; none required by the
  four workflows the brief actually asks for.

The common thread: every one of these came up while researching this
assessment (including from AI-generated advice — see `docs/ai-usage.md`),
and each was evaluated and consciously left out, rather than silently
skipped because it wasn't mentioned.
